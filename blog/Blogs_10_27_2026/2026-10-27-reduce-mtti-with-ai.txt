<h2 id="importance">Why is reducing Mean Time to Investigate (MTTI) important?</h2>
<p>MTTI is the "hidden" metric that dictates the total impact of a cyberattack. If an attacker gains access to a network, every minute spent investigating is a minute the attacker has to move laterally or exfiltrate sensitive data. <a href="https://www.ponemon.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Ponemon Institute research</a> suggests that the average time to identify and contain a breach remains over 200 days, a window that provides adversaries with ample time to cause significant damage.</p>

<p>For midsize businesses, a high MTTI is often fatal to operations. Many organizations operate under the misconception that their existing logs are sufficient, but without an AI layer to interpret them, those logs become noise. Reducing MTTI ensures that security teams are not just "reacting" but are proactively neutralizing threats before they escalate into full-scale incidents.</p>

<div>
<h3>What is MTTI?</h3>
<p>MTTI (Mean Time to Investigate) is a security metric that calculates the average time it takes for a security analyst or automated system to review an alert, determine its validity, and understand the scope of a potential threat.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="how-it-works">How does How to reduce mean time to investigate (MTTI) using AI work?</h2>
<p>AI works by functioning as a force multiplier for the human analyst. Instead of an analyst manually cross-referencing IP addresses, file hashes, and user behaviors across different silos, the AI platform performs this correlation in real-time. By integrating with existing data sources, AI identifies the "signal" within the "noise."</p>

<p>When an alert triggers, an AI-powered system like Vigilense AI automatically pulls relevant context—such as recent user activity, geolocation anomalies, and process execution history—and presents it in a unified dashboard. This eliminates the "swivel-chair" effect where analysts move between five different tools to piece together a single story.</p>

<h2 id="implementation">How to implement AI for faster threat investigation</h2>
<h3>Step 1: Audit your existing data landscape</h3>
<p>Identify where your log data lives—cloud environments, endpoints, firewalls, and SaaS applications. You cannot investigate what you cannot see, so ensure your telemetry is being centralized.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Define your baseline "normal"</h3>
<p>AI requires a baseline to distinguish between normal user behavior and malicious activity. Use tools that automatically learn your environment's unique traffic patterns.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Deploy AI-powered detection layers</h3>
<p>Integrate an AI-driven Managed Detection and Response (MDR) solution. Platforms like Vigilense AI can be deployed rapidly, often in days, to start monitoring your existing data streams immediately.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Automate the triage workflow</h3>
<p>Configure automated playbooks that execute the first three steps of any investigation: enrichment, correlation, and initial scoring. This ensures that when an analyst opens a ticket, it is already 80% investigated.</p>

<h3>Step 5: Continuously refine and tune</h3>
<p>Use the feedback loop from your AI platform to tune detection rules. As your business evolves, your AI should adapt to new threat vectors without requiring massive manual reconfiguration.</p>

<h2 id="mistakes">Common mistakes in threat investigation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ignoring "low" priority alerts:</strong> Attackers often use low-level alerts as a smokescreen for larger activity.</li>
<li><strong>Over-reliance on manual processes:</strong> Relying on spreadsheets or manual log searches is a losing battle against modern automated threats.</li>
<li><strong>Data Silos:</strong> Failing to integrate endpoint, network, and cloud data prevents the AI from building a complete picture.</li>
<li><strong>Lack of Context:</strong> Investigating an alert without knowing the user's role or typical behavior often leads to false positives.</li>
</ul>
