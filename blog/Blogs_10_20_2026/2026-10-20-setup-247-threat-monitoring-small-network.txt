<h2 id="importance">Why is 24/7 threat monitoring important?</h2>
<p>Cyber threats do not adhere to a 9-to-5 schedule. According to <a href="https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IBM’s 2024 Cost of a Data Breach Report</a>, the average time to identify and contain a breach remains over 200 days, often because organizations lack the visibility to spot intruders during non-business hours.</p>

<p>For small businesses, a single breach can be catastrophic. Without 24/7 monitoring, an attacker can gain persistence, move laterally through your network, and exfiltrate sensitive data while your office is empty. Implementing continuous monitoring ensures that you are alerted to suspicious activity the moment it occurs, rather than finding out months later when the damage is already done.</p>

<h3>What is MDR (Managed Detection and Response)?</h3>
<p>MDR is a cybersecurity service that combines human expertise with AI-driven technology to provide 24/7 threat monitoring, investigation, and incident response for organizations that lack an in-house security team.</p>

<h2 id="how-it-works">How does 24/7 threat monitoring work?</h2>
<p>Modern threat monitoring relies on the collection and correlation of telemetry data from across your environment. By aggregating logs from firewalls, endpoints, cloud applications, and identity providers, the system builds a baseline of "normal" behavior.</p>

<p>When an event occurs that deviates from this baseline—such as an administrator account logging in from a foreign country at 3:00 AM—the AI triggers an alert. In advanced setups like <a href="https://vigilense.ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Vigilense AI</a>, the system doesn't just alert you; it investigates the context of the threat to determine if it is a false positive or a legitimate attack, allowing your team to focus only on what truly matters.</p>

<h2 id="implementation">How to implement 24/7 monitoring steps</h2>

<h3>Step 1: Centralize your log data</h3>
<p>You cannot monitor what you cannot see. Ensure that all your key infrastructure—servers, cloud instances, and network gateways—are exporting logs to a central repository.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Establish a baseline of normal activity</h3>
<p>Use AI tools to analyze your network traffic for at least 14 days. This helps the system understand the standard behavior of your users and devices, which is critical for reducing false positives.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Deploy endpoint protection</h3>
<p>Install agents on your workstations and servers that report back to a central management console. These agents provide the visibility needed to stop threats at the source.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Configure automated alerting</h3>
<p>Set up alerts that prioritize high-fidelity signals. Avoid "alert fatigue" by tuning the system to notify you only when specific, high-risk conditions are met.</p>

<h3>Step 5: Define your incident response plan</h3>
<p>Even with 24/7 monitoring, you need a plan for when a threat is confirmed. Document who needs to be contacted, which systems should be isolated, and how you will maintain business continuity.</p>

<h2 id="mistakes">Common mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ignoring endpoint logs:</strong> Focusing only on the perimeter while ignoring internal lateral movement.</li>
<li><strong>Over-alerting:</strong> Setting alerts for every minor event, leading to "alert fatigue" where critical warnings are ignored.</li>
<li><strong>Underestimating the "Human" element:</strong> Believing that software alone can solve every security challenge without a strategy for response.</li>
<li><strong>Lack of data ownership:</strong> Using platforms that lock your data away, making it difficult to perform forensic analysis after an incident.</li>
</ul>
