<h2>What is how much does 24/7 threat monitoring cost?</h2>
<p>24/7 threat monitoring costs typically range from $3,000 to $15,000 per month for managed services, though expenses vary based on data volume, the level of human intervention required, and hidden ingestion fees. Rather than a flat rate, most providers bill based on the complexity of your environment and the amount of security data processed.</p>

<p>Many organizations are surprised to find that the "sticker price" of a security tool is only the beginning. Between licensing, personnel costs, and the often-overlooked cost of data ingestion, the total cost of ownership (TCO) can fluctuate wildly. For a midsize business, the goal is to shift from a high-cost, high-headcount model to an efficient, AI-driven approach that keeps data local and costs predictable.</p>

<h2 id="cost-factors">What factors influence the cost of 24/7 threat monitoring?</h2>
<p>The cost of security is rarely a single line item. When evaluating vendors, you must account for several variables that impact your bottom line.</p>

<h3>Data Ingestion Fees</h3>
<p>Many legacy providers charge based on the amount of data you send to their cloud. As your company grows, your data volume grows, leading to "log inflation" that can cause your monthly bill to skyrocket unexpectedly. At <a href="https://vigilense.ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Vigilense AI</a>, we eliminate this by ensuring your data stays in your infrastructure, avoiding expensive egress and storage fees.</p>

<h3>Personnel and SOC Staffing</h3>
<p>The most expensive part of traditional security is the human element. According to <a href="https://www.gartner.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Gartner research</a>, the global cybersecurity skills shortage remains a top driver of increased costs. Relying on a traditional, human-heavy SOC requires hiring 8–12 analysts to provide true 24/7 coverage, which can easily exceed $1 million annually in salary and benefits.</p>

<div>
<h3>What is Data Ingestion?</h3>
<p>Data ingestion is the process of transporting data from various sources (servers, cloud apps, endpoints) into a centralized security platform for analysis; high-volume ingestion often triggers massive, variable monthly fees in traditional models.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="why-important">Why is 24/7 threat monitoring important for midsize businesses?</h2>
<p>Cybercriminals do not operate on a 9-to-5 schedule. A breach occurring on a Friday night or a holiday can remain undetected for months, allowing attackers to exfiltrate sensitive data or deploy ransomware deep into your network.</p>

<p>For midsize businesses, the impact of a breach is often existential. <a href="https://www.ibm.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IBM’s Cost of a Data Breach Report</a> notes that the average cost of a breach for organizations with fewer than 500 employees is over $3 million. 24/7 monitoring is the difference between a minor incident and a company-ending disaster.</p>

<h2 id="how-it-works">How does 24/7 threat monitoring work?</h2>
<p>Modern threat monitoring operates through a combination of automated detection and expert response. By leveraging AI, platforms can analyze millions of events in real-time, filtering out the "noise" of false positives and escalating only the most critical threats to human analysts.</p>

<p>This "AI-first" approach allows organizations to monitor their environment continuously without needing a massive internal team. The system learns the "normal" behavior of your network and alerts you immediately when something deviates from that baseline.</p>

<h2 id="implementation">How do you implement 24/7 threat monitoring?</h2>
<h3>Step 1: Audit your existing data sources</h3>
<p>Identify which systems are critical, such as cloud identity providers, endpoint protection, and network firewalls. You don't need to reinvent your architecture; you just need to connect the right logs.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Define your risk profile</h3>
<p>Determine what "critical" means for your business. For a retail company, it might be the point-of-sale system; for a healthcare provider, it is patient records.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Select an AI-powered partner</h3>
<p>Choose a platform that prioritizes local data retention to keep costs predictable. Avoid vendors that charge per gigabyte of data ingested.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Deploy and calibrate</h3>
<p>Set up the integration. A modern system should be live in days, not months. Use the first few weeks to tune the AI to your specific environment.</p>

<h3>Step 5: Establish response protocols</h3>
<p>Even with great detection, you need a plan. Who gets called at 3 AM? Ensure your response team is aligned with the alerts generated by your platform.</p>

<h2 id="mistakes">What are common threat monitoring cost mistakes?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Paying for "All-You-Can-Eat" Data:</strong> Over-collecting logs you never use leads to massive, unnecessary storage fees.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring Human Costs:</strong> Assuming a tool alone will solve the problem without considering the time required to manage it.</li>
<li><strong>Vendor Lock-in:</strong> Choosing a platform that makes it impossible to export your data if you need to switch.</li>
<li><strong>Underestimating Integration Time:</strong> Choosing "cheap" legacy tools that take months to deploy, costing you more in consulting fees than the software itself.</li>
</ul>
