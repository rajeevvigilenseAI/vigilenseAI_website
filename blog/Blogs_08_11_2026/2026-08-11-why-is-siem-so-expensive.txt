<h2>What is why is SIEM so expensive?</h2>
<p>SIEM is expensive primarily because of "ingestion-based pricing" models, where vendors charge customers based on the volume of data (gigabytes per day) processed, combined with the high cost of the human expertise required to tune, manage, and monitor these complex systems 24/7.</p>

<p>When you purchase a traditional SIEM, you aren't just paying for software; you are entering a relationship that scales linearly with your data growth. As your business generates more logs, your security bill increases, regardless of whether those logs contain actual threats or just noise. This creates a perverse incentive where companies may reduce their log collection—and therefore their security visibility—to keep costs manageable.</p>

<div>
<h3>What is Ingestion-Based Pricing?</h3>
<p>Ingestion-based pricing is a billing model where security vendors charge organizations based on the total volume of data sent to their platform, often penalizing businesses for increasing their security posture and log retention.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="how-siem-works">How does SIEM work?</h2>
<p>At its core, a SIEM collects log data from across your infrastructure—firewalls, servers, endpoints, and cloud applications—normalizes it, and correlates it to identify patterns that indicate a security incident. The process involves:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Log Collection:</strong> Gathering raw data from various sources.</li>
<li><strong>Normalization:</strong> Converting different log formats into a single, searchable standard.</li>
<li><strong>Correlation:</strong> Using rules to link disparate events into a potential security alert.</li>
<li><strong>Alerting:</strong> Notifying a human analyst to investigate the potential threat.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">What are common SIEM cost mistakes?</h2>
<p>Many organizations fall into traps that artificially inflate their security spending. Avoiding these can save your business thousands annually:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ignoring Data Filtering:</strong> Sending "noisy" logs (like debug-level logs) to the SIEM that provide no security value but increase ingestion costs.</li>
<li><strong>Over-Retention:</strong> Keeping logs for years "just in case" without a clear compliance requirement or security use case.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring "In-Place" Alternatives:</strong> Assuming you must move all data to a third-party cloud to analyze it.</li>
<li><strong>Lack of Automation:</strong> Relying on human analysts to manually triage every single alert, leading to "alert fatigue" and higher labor costs.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Does SIEM require a dedicated team?</h3>
<p>Traditionally, yes. Most SIEMs require a dedicated team of security engineers to tune rules and analysts to review logs. Modern AI-powered solutions like ours remove this requirement by automating the detection and investigation workflow.</p>

<h3>What are ingestion fees?</h3>
<p>Ingestion fees are costs charged by security vendors based on the amount of data (usually in gigabytes) your systems send to their platform for analysis. These fees often make SIEMs prohibitively expensive as your data grows.</p>

<h3>Can I keep my data in my own infrastructure?</h3>
<p>Yes. With modern, decentralized security platforms, your data stays in your infrastructure. This is critical for compliance and avoids the "data gravity" costs associated with moving massive amounts of logs to a vendor's cloud.</p>

<h3>How long does it take to deploy a modern security platform?</h3>
<p>Unlike legacy SIEMs that take months to configure, modern AI-powered detection platforms can typically be live in a matter of days because they integrate directly with your existing infrastructure.</p>

<h3>Is AI-powered detection as effective as a human SOC?</h3>
<p>For most midsize organizations, AI-powered detection is actually more effective. It never sleeps, does not suffer from alert fatigue, and can process millions of events in seconds—tasks that would overwhelm a small human team.</p>

<h3>What is the biggest hidden cost of SIEM?</h3>
<p>The biggest hidden cost is the "human-in-the-loop" expense. Even if you pay for the license, you have to pay for the people to manage the software, respond to the alerts, and perform the forensic investigation.</p>

<h3>Why do traditional SIEMs charge by data volume?</h3>
<p>They charge by volume because their underlying architecture requires massive computing and storage resources in their own cloud to process your data. They pass these infrastructure costs on to you.</p>

<h3>How do I reduce my security spend?</h3>
<p>Focus on platforms that offer flat-rate or asset-based pricing rather than ingestion-based pricing. Additionally, leverage AI to automate the triage process so you don't need to hire more analysts.</p>
