The Changing Landscape of Managed Detection and Response

For midsize organizations, the cybersecurity landscape has become increasingly hostile. According to the Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report, a significant majority of breaches target companies with fewer than 1,000 employees, yet these organizations often lack the budget for a 20-person Security Operations Center (SOC). Traditional MDR providers have historically filled this gap, but they introduced a new problem: the "data tax." By charging per gigabyte for log ingestion, these providers effectively penalize you for having better visibility and more comprehensive security logs.

Vigilense AI disrupts this model by shifting the security paradigm. Instead of moving your logs to our environment, we bring the intelligence to your environment. By querying your existing data where it already lives - whether in BigQuery, OpenSearch, S3, or Snowflake - we eliminate the financial barriers to high-fidelity security monitoring. This approach not only saves significant operational budget but also ensures that your team maintains full sovereignty over sensitive corporate data, a critical requirement for compliance in industries like finance, healthcare, and legal services.

The Financial Case Against Traditional MDR Models

When evaluating MDR providers with no ingestion fees, the primary driver is often cost predictability. Traditional MDR vendors operate on a model that scales linearly with your data volume. As your organization matures and your logging becomes more granular, your security bill spikes. This creates a perverse incentive: the more security data you collect to protect your business, the more you are penalized by your vendor.

Vigilense AI removes this friction. By utilizing a fixed-fee model based on your infrastructure rather than your log volume, we allow you to scale your security visibility without fear of budget overruns. This is particularly vital for midsize businesses that need enterprise-grade protection but have limited room for variable OpEx costs. Our platform essentially acts as a force multiplier, automating the heavy lifting of investigation and response while keeping the underlying data footprint exactly where you want it.

How to Select the Right Managed Detection Service (7 Steps)

Step 1: Audit your current data storage. Before choosing an MDR, identify where your logs reside. If you are already using cloud-native data lakes, prioritize providers that can query these directly.

Step 2: Assess your compliance requirements. Ensure the MDR provider can offer a complete audit trail of every automated action taken by their AI or SOC teams.

Step 3: Evaluate the "Human-in-the-Loop" aspect. AI is powerful, but complex breaches often require human context. Choose a partner that blends automation with expert oversight.

Step 4: Request a transparent pricing model. Avoid providers that hide costs behind complex "per-GB" calculations that become opaque as your environment grows.

Step 5: Test the integration timeline. A good MDR provider should be able to demonstrate a proof-of-concept in your environment within a few business days.

Step 6: Review detection coverage. Does the provider correlate data across identity, network, and endpoints? Siloed monitoring is a vulnerability waiting to happen.

Step 7: Check for vendor lock-in terms. Ensure you can migrate or change services without losing your historical security data or being trapped by proprietary formats.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do most MDR providers charge ingestion fees?

Traditional providers charge ingestion fees because they host your data in their own private cloud. They incur storage and compute costs for every gigabyte of log data you send them. By moving your data into their environment, they create a "walled garden" that makes it difficult for you to leave, while also creating a high-margin revenue stream based on your data volume. Vigilense AI changes this by querying your existing data, removing the need for us to store your logs ourselves.

Is my data secure if it stays in my infrastructure?

Yes, in many ways it is more secure. When you store data in your own infrastructure (like an S3 bucket or Snowflake instance), you retain full control over access policies, encryption keys, and lifecycle management. Vigilense AI only queries the data required for threat detection and response. We do not require you to hand over ownership of your logs or expose them to unauthorized third-party environments. This model adheres to the principle of least privilege, minimizing the attack surface associated with log management.
